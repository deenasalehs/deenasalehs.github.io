Teaching Notes

Math for Social Sciences

Eleven notes covering an introductory mathematics sequence for the social sciences, from functions, limits, and systems of equations through derivatives, optimization, matrix algebra, and the mathematics of saving and borrowing.

Numbers, Inequalities, and the Language of Functions

The number systems a social science student actually uses, how to solve and read an inequality, and the basic vocabulary of a function: domain, range, and the vertical line test.

Functions, Graphs, and Transformations

Special functions such as the piecewise and absolute value function, combining and composing two functions, inverse functions, and how a translation or reflection reshapes a graph without changing its underlying rule.

Lines, Limits, and Continuity

Reading the slope of a line as a marginal cost, then building up to the limit of a function and the continuity that makes the rest of calculus possible.

Parabolas and Systems of Equations

The parabola as a model of revenue and cost, solving linear and nonlinear systems of equations, and two worked applications: market equilibrium with a tax and the break even point of a firm.

The Derivative and What It Measures

From the formal definition of the derivative to the product, quotient, and chain rules, with marginal cost, marginal revenue, and marginal profit as the running example.

Optimization: Finding the Best Possible Outcome

Using the first and second derivative tests to find where a function is increasing, decreasing, or at its best possible value, worked through real revenue, cost, and profit problems.

Exponential and Logarithmic Functions in Finance

Compound interest, continuous compounding, and present value, all built on the exponential and logarithmic functions that make them work.

Functions of Two Variables and Partial Derivatives

What changes when a function depends on two variables instead of one, and how the second partials test finds a maximum, a minimum, or a saddle point.

Sequences, Series, and the Mathematics of Saving

Sequences, infinite series, and the geometric series in particular, the mathematics behind a monthly savings plan, a loan, and a depreciating asset.

Annuities and the Mathematics of Loans

The future and present value of an ordinary annuity and an annuity due, how a loan payment is built from the same formulas, and the sinking fund that saves toward a future replacement cost.

An Introduction to Matrix Algebra

Organizing data into matrices, representing a system of equations as a single matrix equation, solving by row reduction, and using the inverse of a matrix to solve for the unknowns directly.

Keywords: calculus for social sciences functions limits continuity functions and transformations parabolas and systems of equations derivatives and optimization marginal cost revenue exponential logarithmic functions partial derivatives sequences and series annuities and loans matrix algebra
